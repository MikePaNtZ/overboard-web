Bench rig — workshop sheet

Exploratory. Not a page claim, not linked from index.html. This is the wave-0 bench rig described in the BoM: a sensored outrunner on a clamped plate with a known inertia on the shaft. It exists to fit a motor model and to shake out the Pi → CAN → VESC real-time path before a board is bought.

Everything here is drawn in the dashed ghost language, deliberately. None of it is built. When it is, the render that replaces this comes out of the MuJoCo model — not out of a drawing tool — so the picture and the physics cannot drift apart.

A · The rig

One motor, one plate, two clamps, one disc. The only fabrication is five holes in the plate: four for the motor face, one clearance hole for the shaft.

The overhang is load-bearing, not cosmetic

If the plate is clamped flush and the disc sits over the desk, the pendulum upgrade is dead and a shed set screw takes a chunk out of the desk. Clamp it so the rotating mass hangs past the edge with clearance below.

B · What it measures

Two spin-ups — one bare, one with the known disc — are enough to separate the torque constant from the rotor inertia. No torque sensor, no load cell, no reaction fixture.

Same commanded current i in both runs, so:

kt·i = Jrotor·α₁   and   kt·i = (Jrotor + Jdisc)·α₂

Two equations, two unknowns, and J_disc comes from mass and radius. Spin-down from steady speed gives the friction term; winding resistance is a multimeter. That is the whole motor model the simulation needs — and it retires the single largest guess in the current model, the unfitted kt = 0.7 N·m/A.

The other half is the part nobody photographs

While the flywheel spins, the Pi is closing a 500 Hz loop over CAN on a real-time kernel. That is where loop jitter, the timeout_msec watchdog, fault reporting and silent current cutback get characterised. The motor is the excuse; the signal path is the experiment.

C · The replay loop

The rig gets modelled in MuJoCo as a real plant, not a prop. Then the measured current command is played back through the model, and the gap between prediction and reality is the thing worth looking at.

Drive the model with the recorded current, not an idealised step, and the residual localises the error: too little friction, wrong inertia, unmodelled actuation lag, or current the drive silently never delivered. Each of those has a different signature, and each maps to a parameter in the model.

Two requirements move on this, and neither of them needs a board: DR-SIM-1 stops being hand-specified, and SR-SIM-3 gets real noise, latency and quantisation figures instead of placeholders.
